Project: Mobile Version of Shipping Craze

Type: Mobile Game Concept

Description: A mobile adaptation of the board game Shipping Craze. Redesigned UI and mechanics to fit mobile screens while keeping the strategic gameplay intact.

Gameplay Screenshots

Creative Process

Started with wireframes, then iterated designs in Figma. Implemented touch-friendly interactions and simplified rules for mobile users. Conducted playtesting to refine user experience.

Next Steps

Planning to develop a full playable mobile prototype and eventually release it on app stores.

01 — Introduction / Overview

Shipping Craze began as a fast-paced tabletop game where players rolled dice, collected goods, and raced to build the most valuable shipping empire. What began as a small classroom prototype eventually evolved into a full digital installation that combines storytelling, motion, code, and physical performance.

The project explores how the act of “unboxing” can become an artistic experience. In the installation, a custom-built shipping box opens itself, revealing a projected game world — transforming a simple button press into a moment of wonder.

02 — Concept Evolution

The original board game invited players to collect, trade, and compete. Using dice, cards, and play money, players built shipping empires while balancing risk and strategy.

The digital version reimagines this experience through projection and automation. Instead of rolling dice, the player presses an LED button. The box unlocks, light spills out, and the world of Shipping Craze unfolds through motion and sound.

03 — The Idea of the Projected Box

I wanted to explore how a simple cardboard box could become a portal into a digital world. The idea of projection emerging from a physical container — something humble and familiar — became central to the project’s identity.

04 — From Prop to Character

The box is more than an object — it behaves like a character. Every LED glow, mechanical movement, and sound cue was choreographed to make the opening sequence feel dramatic and alive. When the lid begins to lift, the experience builds suspense before the projection bursts into view.

05 — Designing the Experience

My goal was to design more than a game: I wanted to create a moment of anticipation and discovery. The opening of the box becomes the first chapter of the experience. Rather than a simple menu screen, the game begins with a performance.

06 — Hardware Breakdown (Part 1)

  • LED Button: Triggers the entire system.
  • Magnetic Lock: Holds the box closed until activation.
  • Servo Motor: Controls the smooth, timed opening of the lid.
  • Mini Projector: Displays the game on the wall.
  • 12V Power Supply: Powers all hardware components safely.

07 — Hardware Breakdown (Part 2)

Cable management and timing synchronization were essential. All components connect to a centralized power system, ensuring the servo, lock, LED, and projector activate in perfect sequence.

08 — Testing & Collaboration

Early prototypes struggled with servo torque, weight distribution, and timing delays. Through iterative testing, I calibrated the servo angle and lock release to create a seamless opening. The synchronized LED fade became the “magic moment” when the box feels alive.

09 — Developing the Game

The digital version initially began in Unity, but a corrupted file forced me to rebuild everything just days before a deadline. This challenge led me to pivot to GDevelop, which allowed the project to be revived quickly in a no-code environment.

The setback became a turning point — pushing the project toward experimentation and iteration.

10 — Game Design Goals

Major design goals included:

  • Satisfying visual and audio feedback
  • Randomized item drops
  • Rarity-based scoring
  • Tactile UI inspired by packaging labels and shipping materials

11 — Interface & Feedback

The UI emphasizes tactile, playful elements: pressed-in buttons, bouncing icons, and sound effects inspired by tape pulls and cardboard flaps. Warm tones represent gains; cool tones represent losses.

12 — Exhibition Setup

  1. Visitor notices the box glowing.
  2. They press the LED button.
  3. The magnetic lock releases.
  4. The lid opens with light and sound.
  5. The projector displays the game, inviting play.

13 — Experience Design

The installation turns interaction into performance. The emotional arc mirrors opening a package: curiosity → anticipation → reveal.

“The chest doesn’t just hold the game — it reveals it.”

14 — Reflection

Shipping Craze: The Project Box blends physical craft, digital interaction, and narrative design. The experience taught me adaptability, especially when technical setbacks pushed the project in new directions.

At its core, Shipping Craze celebrates anticipation — the universal excitement of opening something new.

“I just want people to press the button, watch the chest open, and think — ‘This could be something really fun.’”
